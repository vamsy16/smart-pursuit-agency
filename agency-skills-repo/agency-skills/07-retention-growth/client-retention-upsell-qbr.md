# Client Retention, QBRs & Upsell Strategy

## Purpose
Retention and expansion are cheaper and more profitable than new sales — a ₹10L/month portfolio is far easier to sustain by growing 5 existing clients than by constantly replacing churned ones.

## When to Trigger
Quarterly (formal QBR), and continuously (upsell-readiness monitoring).

## Inputs Required
`[CLIENT_NAME]`, 1 quarter of performance data, `[CURRENT_SCOPE]`, `[UNMET_NEEDS observed]`

## Execution Prompt
```
Act as a client success strategist preparing a Quarterly Business Review for
[CLIENT_NAME], currently on [CURRENT_SCOPE]. Performance data:
[PASTE QUARTER DATA]. Observed unmet needs: [UNMET_NEEDS]

1. QBR Deck Outline: Quarter Recap (wins + misses, honestly) → Key Learnings
   → Next Quarter Strategic Priorities → Investment Recommendation
   (if applicable).
2. Identify 2 natural upsell opportunities based on [UNMET_NEEDS] or
   performance data (e.g., "SEO is compounding — this is the moment to add
   content production" ) — each framed as solving a problem, not selling
   a service.
3. Draft the QBR talking points/script for presenting the upsell without it
   feeling like a sales pitch.
4. Give me 5 early-warning signs of client dissatisfaction/churn risk to
   monitor between QBRs (response time delays, reduced engagement on calls,
   questioning every invoice, etc.)
```

## Deliverable Template
QBR Deck Outline (4 sections) → 2 Upsell Opportunities (framed as problem-solving) → QBR Talking Points → Churn Risk Early-Warning Checklist

## Premium & QA Notes
Always present misses honestly in the Quarter Recap before wins — clients trust agencies more when they see honest self-assessment; it also pre-empts the client raising it first, which is a much weaker position to be in. Never upsell in the same breath as delivering bad news — separate the conversations.
