# Difficult Client Conversations (Mid-Engagement Friction)

## Purpose
The negotiation-closing skill covers pre-sale objections. This covers what actually happens more often once you're delivering: scope creep requests, missed targets, unrealistic demands, and invoice disputes — handled in a way that protects both the relationship and your margin/boundaries.

## When to Trigger
Whenever one of these situations arises mid-engagement.

## Inputs Required
`[SITUATION_TYPE]` (scope creep / missed target / unrealistic guarantee demand / invoice dispute / relationship strain), `[SPECIFIC_CONTEXT]`

## Execution Prompt
```
Act as a senior client relationship advisor. Situation: [SITUATION_TYPE].
Context: [SPECIFIC_CONTEXT]

1. Reframe the situation in one sentence — what is the client actually
   worried about underneath the surface complaint (usually: fear of wasted
   money, fear of looking bad internally, or loss of control)?
2. Response Script: acknowledge the concern genuinely first (no defensive
   opening), then address it factually, then propose a specific next step —
   never leave a difficult conversation without a concrete next action.
3. For scope creep specifically: a script that says yes to the underlying
   need while pricing/timeline for the additional scope, without sounding
   like "that's not in my contract."
4. For missed targets specifically: a script that owns the miss without
   over-apologizing, explains the specific factor(s), and pivots immediately
   to the corrective plan (data-backed, not vague reassurance).
5. Escalation Threshold: 3 signs this relationship has moved from
   "difficult but salvageable" to "not worth continuing" — and a professional
   off-ramp script for that scenario.
```

## Deliverable Template
Underlying Concern Reframe → Response Script → Scope-Creep-Specific Script → Missed-Target-Specific Script → Escalation/Off-Ramp Criteria + Script

## Premium & QA Notes
Never respond to a difficult message the moment you receive it if you feel defensive — draft the response, wait at least an hour (or overnight for serious situations), then send. Every "we missed the target" conversation must end with a specific corrective action and date, not just an apology — clients judge you on the recovery, not the miss itself.
