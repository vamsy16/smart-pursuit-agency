Document Purpose

Qualified enquiries, not cheap leads

Step 4B is the demand-creation half of Smart Pursuit's Solar acquisition system. Meta reaches people who were not searching for Solar and gives them a reason to start. Step 4C covers Google Search — capturing the demand once it exists.

Objective of this system

Turn people with Solar interest into qualified enquiries that can progress toward site assessments, quotations and installations.

Lead generation vs qualified lead generation

 Lead generationQualified lead generation
DefinitionAny person who submits contact details.A person who could realistically buy: serviceable location, suitable property, meaningful electricity usage, a timeline.
Optimised forVolume and low CPL.Cost per qualified lead, and eventually cost per sale.

Why the second matters more for Solar. A rooftop system is a high-ticket, physically-delivered, considered purchase with a long decision cycle. Every unqualified lead costs a real sales conversation, and often a wasted site visit. A tenant in an apartment, a homeowner with a ₹900 monthly bill, or an enquiry from a city the crew cannot reach are all leads — none of them are opportunities. Solar economics punish volume-chasing faster than almost any other service category.

Section 02

Core Solar Offer

Primary offer · used across the entire Solar system

FREE SOLAR ASSESSMENT

Why not simply ask "Do you want to buy Solar?"

Because on Meta, nobody was asking. A cold-traffic user scrolling past a Solar ad has not researched system sizes, has not checked their roof and does not know whether it makes financial sense for their household. "Do you want to buy Solar?" demands a decision they are not equipped to make — so the honest answer is "not yet", and they scroll on.

The assessment asks a much smaller question: would you like to understand what this would involve for your property? That is answerable immediately. The prospect is requesting information, not committing to a purchase, which is why friction drops — while lead quality does not, because they still have to describe a real property and a real bill.

What it is

A no-cost, no-obligation review of the prospect's property and electricity usage that ends with a clear picture of what Solar would involve for them and what the next step is.

Why it matters

It matches where a first-time Indian Solar buyer actually is — curious, bill-frustrated, unsure about roof suitability and suspicious of quick quotations. It also gives the sales team a legitimate reason to call rather than a cold pitch.

How it works

The same offer appears in the Meta ad, the landing page hero, the form button, the WhatsApp opener and the call script — and in the Google Ads system (Step 4C). One offer everywhere makes channel comparison meaningful and removes the message break between ad and page.

What Smart Pursuit should do

Recommendation Lock "Free Solar Assessment" as the default offer for every Solar account unless the client already has a stronger established offer. Do not run a different offer on Meta than on Google in the same period.

What the assessment should help the prospect understand

  • Whether the property may be suitable for Solar
  • Approximate Solar system requirement
  • Electricity usage considerations
  • Potential savings considerations
  • Rooftop / space considerations
  • Installation considerations
  • Available Solar options
  • Recommended next steps
Compliance note — savings language

"Potential savings" must always be presented as an estimate based on the actual information the prospect provides, never as a guaranteed saving. Savings depend on consumption, tariff slab, generation, shading, system size, net-metering rules and DISCOM policy — all of which vary by state and by household. Write "understand your potential savings", never "save ₹X every month" or "recover your cost in X years". The same restriction applies to subsidy claims: describe eligibility as something to be checked, not promised.

Note on the word "Free": it works here because the assessment genuinely costs the client only time. Never use "free" to describe something that later turns out to be conditional — it damages trust exactly when the sales conversation begins.

Why not twelve small audiences

Audience fragmentation, in plain language: a campaign budget is a single pot of money. Split it across twelve ad sets and each one gets a twelfth. Meta's delivery system needs a certain volume of conversion events per ad set before it stops guessing and starts finding the people who actually convert — and an ad set receiving a trickle of budget never reaches that point. So you end up with twelve audiences all permanently learning, none performing, and a report full of numbers too small to mean anything.

There is a second, subtler cost: the same person often sits in several interest audiences at once. Your own ad sets then compete against each other in the auction, which can raise costs and makes it impossible to say which audience produced the result.

Three ad sets is enough

One broad, one topic-relevant, one adjacent-intent. That covers the realistic hypotheses for a new Solar account while keeping each ad set funded enough to exit learning.

Consolidate before you split

Add an ad set only when there is a specific, testable reason and enough budget to support it. Structure serves measurement — never the other way round.

Starting Hypothesis This three-ad-set structure is a launch design, not a permanent one. In many accounts broad eventually absorbs the budget as interest targeting loses its edge; that outcome should be discovered from the data, not assumed.

Why broad can work. When conversion events are firing correctly, Meta's delivery system uses those signals to find people who resemble the ones already converting — and it can read patterns far beyond what an interest list describes. For Solar, the real buyer signal is not "likes solar energy pages"; it is being a property-owning household with a high bill and the means to spend. No interest checkbox captures that. Broad lets the system look for it.

Why the age range is a hypothesis, not a rule. 28–60 is chosen because it roughly brackets property ownership and decision authority in Indian households — younger users often rent, and the upper end still includes many decision-makers. But that is reasoning, not evidence. Recommendation Run it, then read the age and gender breakdown against qualified leads (not raw leads) after enough volume, and adjust. If 45–60 produces the site visits, the range narrows. Targeting parameters are always adjusted from actual client data, never carried over from another account.

Prerequisite

Broad only works when the tracking works. If lead events are not firing accurately, broad targeting has nothing to learn from and simply buys the cheapest available attention. Verify Section 26 events before relying on this ad set.

Geo Targeting

Solar advertising is location-dependent in a way most services are not. A crew has to physically reach the roof, survey it, install on it and return for service. Geography is a hard constraint, not a preference.

Example — Hyderabad Solar company Example

Possible service areas for a hypothetical installer based in Hyderabad:

HyderabadSecunderabadRangareddyMedchalSangareddy

Illustrative only. Replace entirely with the client's confirmed service list.

Non-negotiable rule

Only target locations the Solar company can genuinely service.

Why irrelevant geographic leads are expensive

CostEffect
Wasted spendEvery out-of-area lead was paid for at full price and can never be sold to.
Wasted sales timeSomeone still has to call, discover the problem and reject the lead. At scale this consumes hours the team should spend on real opportunities.
Corrupted optimisationMeta optimises toward whoever submits forms. If out-of-area users convert cheaply, delivery learns to find more of them — the campaign actively gets worse.
Damaged trustA sales team that keeps receiving unusable leads stops calling quickly, which then hurts the genuinely good leads in the same list.
A rejected prospectSomeone who wanted Solar, enquired in good faith and got told no. That is a poor experience attached to the client's brand.

Implementation notes Recommendation

  • Get the service-area list in writing during onboarding, including any areas served only for large commercial jobs.
  • Use the "people living in this location" option rather than broader location settings, so travellers and recent visitors are excluded.
  • Add explicit location exclusions for nearby districts the team will not travel to.
  • Keep City/Area as a form field (Section 14) so location can be verified at the lead level, not just assumed from targeting.
  • If the client serves multiple distinct cities with different capacity, consider a separate ad set or campaign per city — but only once each has enough budget to learn.
  • Re-confirm the service area every quarter. Installers expand and contract coverage more often than they remember to tell their agency.

Solar Customer Problem / Ad Angles

On Meta the audience is not searching, so the ad must start with their problem rather than our product. These four angles cover the main entry points into a Solar decision.

AngleCustomer problemCore messageExample hook
High Electricity BillElectricity costs are highSolar may reduce dependence on grid electricityIs your electricity bill getting harder to ignore?
Solar SavingsCustomer wants to know if Solar is worth itAssess before purchasingBefore you install Solar, calculate whether it makes sense
Rooftop SpaceCustomer doesn't know if the roof is suitableEvaluate roof / property suitabilityYou may already have the space for Solar
EducationCustomer doesn't understand Solar requirementsEducate before sellingDon't start with panels. Start with electricity usage

When to use each angle

High Electricity Bill

Use as the default cold opener. It is the most widely felt, most immediate problem and needs no Solar knowledge to understand. Strongest in months following tariff increases or peak summer consumption. Keep the language about the bill, not about savings figures.

Solar Savings

Use for people already considering Solar — warmer cold traffic and retargeting. It meets the real objection ("is this actually worth it for me?") and positions the assessment as due diligence rather than a sales step. Handle savings language carefully.

Rooftop Space

Use to pre-qualify. Framing around the roof naturally filters toward independent houses, villas and commercial buildings, and away from apartment tenants. Useful when lead quality — not lead volume — is the problem.

Education

Use to build credibility and feed retargeting. It performs well against the common experience of being quoted a system size with no explanation. Expect more engagement and a slightly longer path to enquiry.

How to run them

Recommendation Launch with at least the Bill angle and one other, so the test tells you something about message and not only about audience. Rotate angles when frequency climbs and performance softens — in a fixed service area, audiences saturate, and a new angle is usually a better response than a new audience.

Solar Creative System

First batch

6–8 strong creatives — not 20 weak ones.

Twenty creatives on a modest budget means each one collects a handful of impressions, none reaches a readable result, and the delivery system spreads spend thinly while it tries to find a winner. Six to eight well-made assets across different formats and angles give each one enough delivery to produce a verdict — and give you a clean answer about which angle works, which is the more valuable finding.

FormatRoleWhy it earns a place in the batch
1. Static imageBaseline workhorseFast to produce, cheap to iterate, easy to read in a scroll. The control against which everything else is measured.
2. CarouselSequential explanationSolar needs a short argument — bill, roof, requirement, next step. Carousel delivers it in steps without demanding video attention.
3. Short videoAttention + retargeting fuelHolds attention longest and creates video-view audiences for retargeting (Section 19). Even a simple text-and-footage edit works.
4. Educational graphicCredibilityExplains the logic of system sizing. Positions the client as an adviser rather than a seller, which changes the tone of the first call.
5. Customer-problem creativeCold entryLeads entirely with the bill or the roof. Highest chance of stopping someone who was not thinking about Solar.
6. Savings / assessment creativeOffer clarityPuts the Free Solar Assessment at the centre for people already weighing it up.

Production standards Recommendation

  • Shoot and design for mobile vertical first — most of this audience is on a phone in Feed, Reels and Stories.
  • Legible headline in the first second. If the hook needs reading twice, it is too long.
  • Design for sound-off. Burn captions into video; never rely on a voiceover to carry the message.
  • Use realistic Indian property imagery — independent houses, villas, small commercial rooftops. Generic foreign stock footage of solar farms reads as inauthentic and attracts the wrong audience.
  • No savings figures, no subsidy promises, no countdown urgency on creative. The offer is clarity, and the compliance position must hold in the image as well as the copy.
  • One idea per creative. A creative testing three angles at once teaches nothing about any of them.

Refresh cadence: in a limited service area, frequency builds quickly. Plan a creative refresh cycle from the start rather than waiting for performance to decay — and refresh the hook, not just the background image.

Landing Page Strategy

Recommended destination: /solar-assessment

Cold Meta traffic should go to a dedicated landing page rather than a homepage. The user did not choose to visit this business — they were interrupted mid-scroll and gave the ad about one second of attention. A homepage answers many questions for many audiences: existing customers, job applicants, vendors, browsers. A cold Solar visitor needs exactly one question answered, immediately: what am I being offered and how do I get it?

Homepage problemEffect on a paid Meta visitor
Message breakThe ad promised a Free Solar Assessment; the homepage says "Welcome". The visitor has to re-orient, and some leave.
Competing actionsNavigation, products, blog, about — every link is an exit from the action we paid for.
Weak measurementIf all traffic lands in one place, you cannot tell which angle the page fails.
Slow loadHomepages carry more weight than a purpose-built page; on mobile data this costs visitors before render.

Why the form should not be unnecessarily long

Every additional field costs submissions, and most of that loss happens on a phone with a thumb keyboard. But a shorter form is not automatically better — a two-field form maximises leads and minimises usable information, which shifts the entire qualification burden onto the sales team's call time. The correct length is the minimum needed to contact the person and decide who to call first. Test the rule: if the sales team does not use a field to prioritise or prepare, remove it.

Privacy note: collect only what is needed, state clearly how the details will be used, and handle lead data in line with applicable Indian data-protection requirements. This applies equally to website forms, Instant Forms and WhatsApp conversations.

Section 15

Lead Qualification

Two additional questions convert a contact list into a prioritised pipeline.

Average monthly electricity bill

Below ₹2,000₹2,000–₹5,000₹5,000–₹10,000₹10,000–₹20,000₹20,000+

A proxy for consumption, likely system size and financial logic. Bands are easier to answer honestly than an exact figure.

Solar purchase timeline

Just exploringWithin 3 monthsWithin 6 monthsReady to discuss now

Separates this month's revenue from next quarter's nurture list.

How these help the sales team

  • Call order. A ₹20,000+ bill with a "ready to discuss now" timeline is called before a "just exploring" enquiry, every time. Without these fields, the team calls in the order leads arrive — which is effectively random.
  • Call preparation. Knowing the bill band and property type before dialling makes the first conversation specific instead of exploratory.
  • Honest expectation-setting with the client. A week with fewer leads but a higher share of high-bill, near-term enquiries is a better week. These fields let us show that rather than argue it.
  • Feedback into targeting. Over time, comparing bill bands and timelines across ad sets reveals which audience actually produces buyers — the input for Section 24's optimisation decisions.
Two cautions

Self-reported answers are indicative, not verified — some people underestimate their bill, and "just exploring" is sometimes politeness rather than truth. Also weigh the cost: these two fields will reduce raw submissions. That is an accepted trade for a pipeline the sales team can actually work. Measure the impact on qualified leads, not total leads, before deciding whether to keep them.

Lead Quality Framework

HOT LEAD
  • High electricity bill
  • Suitable property
  • Serviceable location
  • 0–3 month timeline
  • Interested in a site assessment or quotation

Handling: call first, call fast, aim to book the site assessment on the first conversation.

WARM LEAD
  • Suitable property
  • Genuine interest
  • 3–6 month timeline
  • Needs additional information

Handling: answer the open questions, send the assessment summary, set a specific follow-up date. Most Solar revenue sits in this band over time.

COLD LEAD
  • Just researching
  • Low usage
  • Outside service area
  • No clear purchase intent

Handling: one courteous contact, then nurture — not repeated calling. Out-of-area leads are closed immediately and fed back into targeting review.

Important

These are internal prioritisation categories, not guaranteed indicators of sales. A "hot" lead can go silent; a "cold" researcher can install six months later after a subsidy decision or a family conversation. The framework exists to allocate limited sales attention sensibly, not to predict outcomes. Review the definitions once real close-rate data exists — the client's actual buyers may not match these assumptions.

Why speed-to-lead matters

A Solar enquiry is submitted in a moment of active interest. Minutes later the person is doing something else; hours later they may have filled two competitors' forms as well. Speed affects three things directly: connection rate (they still remember enquiring and are still near the phone), competitive position (the first credible caller frames the comparison), and perceived professionalism (a fast, prepared call signals a company that will also turn up for the installation).

Treat 5–10 minutes as a target, not a law

It is a recommended operating target, not a universal rule, and it must be agreed against the client's real staffing. A team of two cannot answer instantly at 10pm, and a promise nobody can keep is worse than an honest standard. Agree in writing: who responds, in what window, during which hours, and what happens to leads that arrive outside them. Then measure it — average response time and contact-attempt count belong in the weekly report, because advertising cannot fix an unanswered lead.

Section 20

Budget Testing

Illustration only Example

If the monthly Meta ad budget is ₹20,000, an example starting allocation is roughly ₹600–₹700 per day.

This is not a budget recommendation for every client

The figure above is arithmetic on a hypothetical number, shown to demonstrate how a monthly budget converts to a daily one. It is not a benchmark, not a minimum, and not a prediction of what it will produce.

Budget depends on

  • Service area size
  • Average Solar order value
  • Sales capacity — site assessments per week
  • Competition in the market
  • Historical CPL, once it exists
  • Lead-to-sale rate
  • Revenue target
Never promise

A fixed number of leads for a fixed budget.

No agency can know lead volume before the account runs, because it depends on auction prices, competitors, seasonality, creative performance and the client's own response speed. Recommendation Instead, agree a phased approach: Test — run a controlled budget long enough to gather creative, audience and conversion data. Stabilise — fix lead quality and the landing page until the sales team trusts the pipeline. Scale — increase spend only when qualified-lead economics work against order value and the client has capacity to handle more site assessments. Scale when the constraint is demand — never while it is still quality or follow-up.

Section 21

Testing Framework

#VariableWhat a test here answers
1CreativeWhich format and visual stops the scroll. Usually the highest-impact variable on Meta.
2HookWhich problem framing — bill, roof, savings, education — resonates in this market.
3OfferWhether the assessment framing is right, or needs sharpening for this client.
4AudienceBroad vs Solar interest vs home improvement, judged on qualified leads.
5Landing pageWhether the page converts the traffic the ads are already producing.
6Lead qualificationWhether form fields and definitions are separating real opportunities correctly.
Rule

Do not change everything at the same time.

Why controlled testing matters. Change the creative, the audience and the landing page in one week and the result — better or worse — teaches nothing, because there is no way to attribute it. Worse, you will likely keep all three changes, including the one that hurt. Solar accounts make this harder than most: volumes are modest, the sales cycle is long, and the metric that matters most (qualified leads, then site visits) arrives slowest. So changes need patience.

Recommendation One meaningful variable at a time. Give it enough time and volume to produce a readable result rather than judging on a noisy day or two. Write down what you changed, when, and why — an account without a change log cannot explain its own history. And test in order of impact: creative and hook first, then audience, then page, then form.

Section 23

The Most Important Metric

Cost per Lead is the easiest number to improve and the easiest to be fooled by. Loosen targeting, shorten the form, soften the offer — CPL falls immediately, and the pipeline can get worse at the same time.

The illustration that makes this concrete Example

A ₹150 lead that never answers the phone may be worth less than a ₹500 lead that becomes a site visit. On a CPL report the first looks three times better. In the business, only the second can become revenue.

The metric hierarchy Smart Pursuit works toward

CPLEasy to measure, easiest to mislead with
↓
COST PER QUALIFIED LEADFirst honest efficiency number
↓
COST PER SITE VISITProves the lead was real and reachable
↓

↓
COST PER SALEThe number the client's business actually runs on
↓

How to apply this

Move one step down the hierarchy as soon as the data allows. In week one only CPL exists; within weeks, qualified-lead data should come back from the client's CRM; over a full sales cycle, site visits, quotations and sales become measurable. Report at the deepest level available and say so explicitly — and never present a deeper metric as achieved when it is still being estimated. This progression is what separates managing ads from managing customer acquisition.

Section 24

Optimization Rules

Diagnose at the stage that is actually failing. Each symptom points at a specific part of the system.

If CTR is low

  • Hook — first line and first second
  • Creative — format, imagery, legibility
  • Message — problem framing
  • Audience — wrong people, or saturated frequency

If CTR is good but landing-page conversion is low

  • Landing page structure and clarity
  • Offer strength
  • Page speed
  • CTA visibility and repetition
  • Form length
  • Message match with the ad

If leads are cheap but poor quality

  • Targeting — too broad or wrong interests
  • Lead-form questions — add qualifiers
  • Ad messaging — attracting curiosity, not intent
  • Location targeting and exclusions
  • Qualification definitions

If leads are good but sales are poor

  • Speed-to-lead
  • Sales follow-up persistence
  • Lead qualification accuracy
  • Pricing position
  • Site assessment process
  • Sales-team performance
Important

Do not automatically blame advertising when downstream sales performance is poor — and do not automatically blame the client either. Follow the evidence: if leads are in-area, own suitable properties, report meaningful bills and answer the phone, the constraint is not the campaign. Bring the data — response times, contact attempts, connection rates, site-visit booking rate — and agree the fix jointly. This conversation is far easier when the reporting in Section 27 has been shared from week one.

Section 25

Message Match

Message match means the promise does not change as the person moves through the funnel. If the ad says "Get a Free Solar Assessment", the landing page must immediately say "Get Your Free Solar Assessment" — in the hero, in the same words, before any scrolling.

AD PROMISE"Get a Free Solar Assessment"
↓
LANDING PAGE PROMISE"Get Your Free Solar Assessment"
↓
FORMButton: "Get My Free Solar Assessment"
↓
SALES CONVERSATION"You requested a free assessment — let's go through your property"

Why the chain must hold. Every break costs people. A visitor who lands on a generic homepage with unrelated messaging has to work out whether they are in the right place — and a share of them simply leave, after we have already paid for the click. A visitor who submits a form expecting an assessment and receives a hard price pitch feels misled, which damages the conversation at the exact moment trust is being built.

There is a measurement benefit too: when the promise is identical at every stage, a drop-off can be attributed to a specific step rather than to confusion spread across all of them. Recommendation Before any launch, read the ad and the page side by side on a phone and check that the same five words appear in both. It is the cheapest conversion fix available.
