Purpose of Google Ads

Meta and Google do two different jobs in the same funnel. Confusing the two is the most common reason a Solar ad budget underperforms.

Meta Ads

Creates awareness and demand. The user was not thinking about Solar. An ad in the feed interrupts them with a reason to think about it: rising electricity bills, rooftop space sitting idle, subsidy awareness, neighbours installing panels.

Job: make Solar feel relevant to someone who wasn't looking for it.

Google Ads

Captures people already searching for Solar. The user has typed the need themselves. We are not persuading them that Solar exists — we are competing to be the provider they contact.

Job: be present, relevant and easy to contact at the moment of search.

The Google Ads Objective

Capture relevant, high-intent Solar searches and convert them into qualified enquiries.

Note the two qualifiers. Relevant rules out the large volume of Solar searches made by job seekers, students and researchers. Qualified rules out enquiries that can never buy — wrong city, tenant with no roof rights, budget far below a real system cost.

The Google Ads Offer

Core Offer · used across the entire Solar system

FREE SOLAR ASSESSMENT

What it is

A no-cost, no-obligation review of the prospect's property and electricity usage that ends with a clear picture of what Solar would involve for them and what the next step is.

Why it matters

"Get a quote" asks for a decision the prospect is not ready to make. "Understand your requirement" matches where a first-time Solar buyer actually is. It lowers the psychological cost of enquiring without lowering lead quality, because the person still has to describe a real property and a real bill.

How it works

The same offer appears in the Meta ad, the Google ad, the landing page hero, the form button, the WhatsApp opener and the sales call script. One offer repeated everywhere is easier to measure, easier to script for the client's sales team, and removes the message break between ad and page.

What Smart Pursuit should do

Recommendation Lock "Free Solar Assessment" as the default offer for every Solar account unless the client already has a stronger, established offer. Never run a different offer on Google than on Meta in the same period — it makes channel comparison meaningless.

What the prospect should understand

"Tell us about your property and electricity usage, and we'll help you understand your Solar requirements and next steps."

What the assessment can cover

  • Property suitability — independent house, villa, apartment, factory shed, commercial roof
  • Approximate Solar requirement — indicative system size range
  • Electricity usage — monthly units and bill pattern
  • Rooftop considerations — usable area, shading, structure, access
  • Potential savings considerations — the factors that influence savings
  • Installation considerations — timelines, approvals, metering
  • Available options — on-grid / hybrid / off-grid, component tiers
  • Next steps — site assessment, then quotation
Compliance note — savings language

Never present potential savings as guaranteed savings. Savings depend on consumption, tariff slab, generation, shading, system size, net-metering rules and DISCOM policy, all of which vary by state and by household. Write "understand your potential savings", never "save ₹X every month" or "recover cost in X years". The same restriction applies to subsidy claims: describe availability and eligibility as something to be checked, not promised.

Why one offer across the system

BenefitPractical effect
Clean channel comparisonIf Meta and Google run the same offer, differences in cost per qualified lead reflect the channel, not the offer.
Consistent sales scriptThe client's team answers every lead the same way, whatever the source.
Faster learningOne offer collects data faster than three competing offers splitting the same budget.
Message matchAd promise and landing page promise are identical, which supports both conversion rate and ad relevance.

Primary Google Campaign

Recommended starting campaign

SEARCH CAMPAIGN

Recommendation Start with a single Search campaign. Not Performance Max, not Display, not Demand Gen. Search is the only campaign type where we can see the exact query a person typed, exclude the queries we don't want, and control spend at the intent level. For a new Solar account with no conversion history, that control is worth more than reach.

Why Search fits Solar specifically

  • Solar is a considered, high-ticket purchase. A residential rooftop system is a significant household spend, so buyers research actively and search deliberately rather than impulse-buying from a feed.
  • It is a local service. Installation requires a physical visit, so searches carry strong geographic intent — exactly what Search targeting handles well.
  • Demand already exists. Rising tariffs, rooftop subsidy awareness and visible neighbourhood installations push people to search on their own.
  • Intent is readable. "solar installer near me" and "solar training course" are completely different people, and Search lets us pay for only one of them.

What people actually search

solar installation near merooftop solar installationsolar company near me solar panels for homesolar installation Hyderabad

Example Illustrative queries used throughout this document to teach structure. Real keyword sets must be built per client from Google Keyword Planner, the client's service area and, once live, the account's own Search Terms report.

The central caution

Search intent varies enormously inside the single word "solar". The same word is typed by a homeowner with a ₹6,000 monthly bill, an ITI student looking for a course, an engineer looking for a job, and a school student writing a project report. Keyword quality — not keyword volume — decides whether a Solar Search campaign works.

Search Intent Framework

Every Solar keyword Smart Pursuit considers must first be sorted into one of four intent levels. This framework decides what we bid on, what we postpone, and what we exclude outright.

Level 1 — High Commercial IntentBid first · Highest priority
solar installation near merooftop solar installationsolar installer near me solar company near merooftop solar companysolar panel installation residential solar installation

These searches can indicate that the user is actively looking for a Solar provider or an installation, not just information. The words "installation", "installer", "company" and "near me" all point at hiring someone rather than learning something. Competition and cost per click are usually highest here — and that is normally acceptable, because this is the traffic most likely to become a site assessment.

Note: "can indicate" is deliberate. Intent is a probability, not a certainty. Only the Search Terms report and the client's qualification notes confirm it.

Level 2 — Product / Solution IntentBid second · Valuable with context
rooftop solar panelssolar panels for homehome solar system residential solar systemsolar power system for home5kw solar system

These users are researching a purchase rather than requesting one. They are working out what they need — panel type, system size, what "5kW" means for their bill. They can be very valuable, because the next step after understanding the product is usually contacting an installer. Their value depends on context: a person searching "5kw solar system" with a large independent house is closer to buying than someone comparing panel brands out of curiosity.

Handling: route to an educational-but-converting page (see Section 18), and expect a longer lead-to-sale cycle than Level 1.

Level 3 — Informational IntentPostpone when budget is limited
how does rooftop solar worksolar panel benefitshow much electricity does solar generate solar panel maintenancesolar system calculation

These users are earlier in the journey. Some will buy eventually; most are reading. On a limited budget they should be approached carefully — not because they are worthless, but because they consume clicks that Level 1 searches would convert faster. There is also a hidden trap: "solar panel maintenance" often comes from people who already own a system, and "solar system calculation" is frequently a student query.

Better home for this intent: SEO content and Meta retargeting, not paid Search spend, until the account has proven Level 1 economics.

Level 4 — Low / Irrelevant IntentExclude before launch
solar jobssolar trainingsolar coursesolar careers solar government jobssolar manufacturing jobssolar project reportsolar science project

These searches share our vocabulary but none of our commercial purpose. In India, Solar employment and Solar training queries carry heavy volume — an installer account left unguarded will attract clicks from job seekers, ITI and diploma students, and school project researchers. None of them can buy a rooftop system. Worse, this traffic often clicks well, which makes a report look healthy while producing zero enquiries.

Action: these become the seed list for the negative keyword system in Section 12, added before the first rupee is spent.

How to use this framework

Sort every candidate keyword into a level before it enters the account. Budget flows top-down: Level 1 fully funded, Level 2 funded once Level 1 is stable, Level 3 only with evidence, Level 4 never.

Naming convention: AGENCY_NICHE_CHANNEL_OBJECTIVE_VERSION. Versioning in the name keeps historical structures readable when V2 launches.

Recommended initial ad groups

#Ad GroupCore intent it serves

4Home Solar"I am considering Solar for my home."
5Local / City Solar"I want a Solar company in my city."

Recommendation Five ad groups is the starting structure, not a permanent one. It changes when data justifies change.

Why not thirty tiny ad groups

Single-keyword ad groups look disciplined on a spreadsheet and behave badly in a small account. Every ad group needs enough impressions and conversions for Google's systems — and for us — to learn anything. Split a modest Solar budget across thirty ad groups and each one collects a handful of clicks per week: no ad group ever reaches statistical meaning, responsive search ads never finish learning which asset combinations work, automated bidding has nothing to optimise against, and the weekly report becomes thirty rows of noise.

The balance we are managing

Organization

Keywords in an ad group should share one intent so a single set of headlines can honestly answer all of them. This drives relevance, Quality Score and message match.

Sufficient conversion / data volume

Each ad group needs enough traffic to produce readable signal. Too granular and every decision becomes a guess dressed as a metric.

Working rule

Split an ad group only when (a) its search terms clearly represent a different promise that needs different ad copy, and (b) it is already producing enough volume to survive on its own. Organisation serves measurement — never the other way round.

Campaign settings at launch Recommendation

SettingStarting position & why
NetworksSearch only. Turn off Search Partners and Display Expansion initially so all data is clean Google Search data.
LocationsOnly the cities/areas the client genuinely services. Use "Presence: people in or regularly in your targeted locations" — not "interest" — to avoid out-of-area enquiries.
LanguagesEnglish plus the relevant regional language where the client's pages and sales team can actually handle it.
BiddingStarting Hypothesis Begin on Maximise Clicks with a CPC cap or Manual CPC while conversion tracking proves itself; move to Maximise Conversions / Target CPA only after a meaningful conversion history exists.

Solar Installation

solar installationsolar installation near merooftop solar installation solar panel installationsolar installersolar installers near merooftop solar installer

Primary intent: "I need someone to install Solar."

Why this theme is high value

The word "installation" describes a service being hired, not a product being studied. Someone typing it has already accepted that Solar is for them; the open question is who does the work. That is the shortest remaining distance to a site assessment of any keyword group in the account.

What to expect

Starting Hypothesis Highest CPC in the account and the highest share of genuine enquiries. We should be willing to pay more per click here than anywhere else, because cost per qualified lead — not cost per click — is the measure that matters.

Copy angle

Lead with the service and the location: "Rooftop Solar Installation in Hyderabad". Do not lead with education — this person is past that stage.

Landing page

/solar-installation or the city variant. Contact options visible above the fold on mobile.

Watch-out

"Installation" also attracts installation workers — "solar installation technician", "solar installation job", "solar installation training". Employment and education negatives (Section 12) must be live before this ad group runs.

Section 09 · Ad Group 4

Home Solar

solar panels for homehome solar systemresidential solar residential solar panelssolar system for housesolar for independent house

Intent: "I am considering Solar for my home."

Why residential deserves its own theme

Residential and commercial Solar are different products sold to different buyers with different objections, ticket sizes, decision cycles and approval processes. A homeowner asks about bill reduction, roof space and subsidy eligibility. A factory owner asks about sanctioned load, CAPEX vs OPEX and payback on the balance sheet. One set of headlines cannot serve both convincingly.

When to split it out

Recommendation Separate it when volume justifies it — when home-specific search terms are consistently appearing and can support their own copy and page. Before that, keep them together to protect data volume (Section 05).

Qualification reality in India

Residential Solar traffic includes a meaningful share of apartment residents and tenants who cannot authorise a rooftop installation. This is not a campaign fault — it is a qualification step. The form and the call script must establish property type and roof ownership early, and "independent house" style keywords tend to pre-filter better than generic "solar for home".

Landing page

/residential-solar — home-specific imagery, home-specific FAQs, home-specific assessment form fields (monthly bill, roof area, house type).

Non-negotiable rule

Only use locations the client actually services. Advertising "Solar Installation in Warangal" when the client's team cannot travel there produces enquiries that must be rejected — wasted spend, a frustrated prospect, and a sales team that starts distrusting the leads we send. Get a written service-area list during onboarding and build the location keyword set strictly from it.

Why local intent matters so much for installers

  • The service is physically delivered. A crew has to reach the roof. Geography is a hard constraint, not a preference.
  • City wording signals readiness. Adding a city name to a query usually means the person is looking for a provider to contact now, not reading about technology.
  • Relevance is easy to win. Many national aggregators run generic copy. A headline naming the user's city is often the most relevant result on the page.
  • Approvals and DISCOM processes are local. Net metering and subsidy processing differ by state and utility; a local provider is genuinely more useful, and the copy can say so.
  • Cost control. Tight geography prevents spend leaking into cities that can never be serviced.

City keywords vs location targeting

These are two separate controls and both are needed. Location targeting decides who is eligible to see the ad. City keywords let us write copy that names the city, which lifts click-through and lets us send traffic to a matching city page. A person in Hyderabad searching "solar installation near me" is caught by location targeting; a person searching "solar installation Hyderabad" from anywhere is caught by the keyword.

Keyword Match Types

Match type controls how far Google may stray from the words we typed. It is a control dial for reach versus precision — not a ranking of good and bad.

Exact Match Core at launch

Useful for tightly controlled high-intent searches. Matches the keyword and close variants with the same meaning. Highest precision, narrowest reach, usually the highest CPC — and normally the best place to spend a limited Solar budget first.

Example: [solar installation hyderabad]

Phrase Match Core at launch

Useful for controlled variations around a search theme. Matches queries that include the meaning of the phrase with additional words around it — "best solar installation hyderabad cost", "solar installation hyderabad for home". Wider than exact, still anchored to intent.

Example: "solar installation hyderabad"

Broad Match Test later

Broad match is not inherently bad. It is a discovery tool that finds converting queries we never thought to write down, and it is the match type Google's automated bidding works with most freely. It simply requires infrastructure before it can be trusted.

Test broad match once all four are true:

  • Conversion tracking is reliable — form, call and key WhatsApp events firing accurately and de-duplicated.
  • Negative keywords are maintained — the Solar-specific job/course/project lists are live and being extended.
  • Enough data exists — a conversion history the bidding can actually learn from.
  • Search-term monitoring is active — someone is genuinely reviewing terms on a fixed schedule, not occasionally.

Comparison

Match typeReachControlBest use in a Solar account
[exact]LowHighProven, high-intent, city-specific installation queries.
"phrase"MediumMediumTheme coverage around installation, company and rooftop terms.
broadHighLowControlled discovery once tracking, negatives and data are in place.
Match type strategy should evolve

The launch mix is a hypothesis, not a doctrine. Starting Hypothesis Begin with exact + phrase on Level 1 and Level 2 themes. As the Search Terms report reveals what really converts, promote winning queries to exact, prune phrase keywords that only attract noise, and open a small, ring-fenced broad-match test — ideally in its own ad group or campaign so its spend and results stay separately readable. Let the account's own data set the mix.

How to run the list

PracticeDetail

Two cautions

1. Negatives must evolve. The list above is a launch seed built from expected Indian Solar search behaviour — a Starting Hypothesis, not a finished asset. The real list is written by the account's own Search Terms report.

2. Do not blindly exclude every unusual search. An odd query is not automatically a bad one. "solar without subsidy", "solar for 3bhk", "solar emi" and "solar for borewell" all look unusual and all may be genuine buyers. Exclude on evidence of low value — clicks with no enquiries, or enquiries the sales team consistently rejects — not on first impression.

Why search terms beat assumptions

  • Real vocabulary. Indian Solar buyers search in ways a keyword planner rarely surfaces — "solar for 2bhk", "solar panel price 3kw", mixed-language and transliterated queries, locality names we didn't list.
  • It reveals hidden waste. A keyword can look fine at the keyword level while most of its spend goes to one bad query underneath it.
  • It validates intent levels. Our four-level framework is a prediction; search terms tell us whether the prediction held.
  • It compounds. Every review makes the next week's traffic cleaner. This is the main engine of improvement in the first three months.
Review discipline Recommendation

Fixed weekly slot. Sort by cost descending, not by impressions. Judge on enquiries and qualified enquiries, not clicks. Give a term enough clicks before killing it — a single click with no conversion proves nothing. Log every negative added and every keyword promoted with the date and reason, so the account has a decision history rather than a mystery.

How Google uses these

Google combines headlines dynamically, showing different permutations to different searchers and learning which combinations perform. This has two consequences. First, every headline must make sense next to every other headline — no half-sentences that only work in one order. Second, avoid stuffing every headline with the same keyword. Fifteen variations of "Solar Installation Hyderabad" give the system nothing to test and read as spam to the user. Cover distinct angles instead: service, location, offer, benefit, segment, CTA. Pin only when something must always appear (a legal requirement or the city name), and accept that heavy pinning reduces the system's ability to learn.

What descriptions are actually for

Headlines win attention; descriptions filter and reinforce. In a lead-generation account, a description that attracts fewer but better clicks is worth more than one that attracts everyone.

  • Qualifying the click. "home or business", "electricity usage", "property type", "rooftop considerations" — each phrase tells a job seeker or student that this page is not for them, and tells a homeowner that it is.
  • Setting expectations. "before requesting a quotation" makes clear this is an assessment, not an instant price. Fewer disappointed form-fills, fewer angry calls.
  • Reinforcing intent. Repeating the searcher's own frame ("rooftop Solar installation") confirms they are in the right place.
  • Naming the next step. "Enquire today", "Submit your details and discuss your requirements" — the user knows exactly what happens after the click.

Craft notes: write each description to stand alone, since Google may show only one. Keep the most important words early in case of truncation on mobile. Avoid savings figures, subsidy promises and urgency tricks.

Location in Ad Copy

Naming the service location does three things at once: it raises relevance for city searches, it pre-qualifies the click by discouraging out-of-area users, and it signals a local business rather than a national lead aggregator — which matters to a buyer who wants someone accountable for the roof.

Implementation notes

Only put a city in copy if location targeting and the landing page agree with it. Dynamic location insertion can help at scale but must be tested — a mis-inserted locality name damages credibility faster than a generic headline.

Why the homepage is the wrong default

  • It answers nobody specifically. A homepage serves existing customers, job applicants, vendors and buyers at once, so the paid visitor has to find their own path.

Call Intent

Some Solar searches carry strong call intent. A person typing solar installer near me on a phone frequently wants to speak to someone now, not complete a form and wait. Forcing that person through a form is friction we chose to add.

Recommended assets Recommendation

Why calls must be measured, not assumed

If calls are untracked, the campaign is judged on form fills alone. Every ad group whose audience prefers to phone looks like a failure — and the natural response is to cut exactly the highest-intent keywords in the account. Tracked calls also let automated bidding optimise toward them, and let us hold the sales side accountable: answer rate and missed-call rate become visible numbers instead of an invisible leak. A missed call on a high-intent Solar keyword is a paid click thrown away.

Operational prerequisite: agree with the client, in writing, who answers the phone, during which hours, and what happens to missed calls. Advertising cannot fix an unanswered phone.

WhatsApp is a default communication channel in India and often the lowest-friction way for a prospect to start a conversation — no form, no phone call, no commitment. Google Conversion Tracking

Conversion tracking is what the bidding algorithm learns from. Tell it the wrong thing and it will get very good at producing the wrong result.

Primary conversions

  • Lead form submission
  • Qualified enquiry
  • Phone call (above a minimum duration)
  • Site assessment booking

Counted as conversions. Used for bidding.

Secondary conversions

  • WhatsApp click
  • Contact page visit
  • Form start
  • Calculator completion

Observation only. Not used for bidding.

Why every interaction cannot be a conversion

Google's automated bidding buys more of whatever we call a conversion. If a contact-page visit and a booked site assessment are both "conversions", the system treats them as equally valuable and will happily buy a thousand page views instead of ten assessments — and the report will show a spectacular cost per conversion that produces no revenue. Counting everything also destroys the cost-per-lead number the client cares about, because the denominator is full of things that are not leads.

Section 24

Budget Strategy

There is no universal correct Solar budget. It depends on:

  • Search volume in the target area
  • Location — metro CPCs differ from tier-2 CPCs
  • Competition — local installers plus national aggregators
  • Average Solar order value
  • Sales capacity — how many site assessments the team can actually do per week
  • Target revenue
  • Historical performance, once it exists
Never promise this

"₹20,000 = X leads" — No agency can know this before the account has run. Any such promise is either a guess presented as a fact or a commitment we cannot control, since lead volume depends on auction prices, competitors, seasonality and the client's own sales response.

Say this instead

Start with a controlled test budget, collect search and conversion data, then scale according to qualified lead economics.

Budget follows evidence

PhasePurposeWhat must be true before moving on
TestLearn which searches exist, convert and qualifyTracking verified; search terms reviewed; a first read on cost per qualified lead
StabiliseClean the traffic and fix the pageNegatives working; lead quality acceptable to the sales team; landing page converting predictably
ScaleIncrease spend on what worksQualified-lead economics make sense against order value; sales team has capacity to handle more assessments

Starting Hypothesis A common starting approach is to set a daily budget that can realistically produce a few clicks per day on Level 1 keywords in the target city, hold it steady long enough to gather search-term and conversion data, and change one major variable at a time. The correct figure is derived per client from local CPCs and the client's sales capacity — not copied from another account. Scale only when the constraint is demand, never when it is still lead quality or sales follow-up.

Section 32

Smart Pursuit Google Ads Principle

Operating principle

The objective is not to get as many clicks as possible. The objective is to capture relevant Solar search demand and turn it into qualified business opportunities.

Clicks are an intermediate metric. They are easy to buy, easy to increase and easy to mistake for progress. Lowering bids, loosening match types or writing vaguer headlines will all raise click volume — and every one of those moves can simultaneously reduce qualified enquiries. A report showing more clicks and fewer site assessments is a report of decline, presented as growth.

Clicks matter only as a step: a paid permission to present the offer. Their value is entirely determined by what happens next. So Smart Pursuit judges a Solar account in this order — qualified enquiries first, assessments booked second, sales third, clicks last — and any optimisation that improves clicks while worsening the first three is rejected, regardless of how good it looks on the surface of the dashboard.
