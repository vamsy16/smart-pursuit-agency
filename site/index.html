Design of an autonomous one-person agency

The growthdepartment is theoperating system.

Smart Pursuit is not a digital marketing agency that uses AI. It is a manufacturing line with one senior operator: strategy and trust stay human; research, production, measurement, reporting and money-chasing are made by a machine that never sleeps and never forgets a file.

So the design does something unusual for an "AI agency" plan: it starts from the founder's calendar and works backwards. Every module below has to justify itself by either shrinking the day or widening a client's trust. If it does neither, it doesn't get built.

01 · Architecture

Three planes.
One filesystem.

No CRM subscription, no dashboard vendor, no bot farm. The company runs on text files in a git repository that an AI kernel reads the way an employee reads a handbook — and on scheduled jobs that write results back. The diff is the timesheet; the commit message is the audit trail.

AUTONOMY L0 — proposes L2 — acts freely

Client-facing work sits at L0: the system drafts into 10-outbox/, a human glances, a human sends. Internal work — research, briefs, audits, filing, gate-scoring — runs at L2, untouched. Agencies that automate the first column are the ones that end up in refunds and screenshots.

02 · The six engines

Demand, sale, delivery,
proof, retention — and a filter.

Each engine is a folder: a playbook, its templates, its own data. They run independently, so you can build one per week and have it working before the next exists. Engine 00 exists because "general digital marketing" is a fine front door and a fatal inside.

00

Triage & Fit

Every enquiry is scored on nine fields — budget, LTV, decision access, urgency, maturity — before it gets a calendar slot. A gets a call. B gets a paid Sprint. C gets a polite one-message no that sends them to a bench partner.

If a daemon dies, nothing client-facing stops — because the manual version of every ritual is already written in its playbook. The system degrades to a documented routine, never to silence. That is the difference between an operating system and a fragile automation.

One payroll line.

"At ₹2.5L we can't include the twelve social posts" keeps the floor intact and keeps the client's respect. "Okay, ₹2L" starts a relationship where you are quietly over-billed and they are quietly disappointed. The floor is not a number you defend to clients — it's a number you defend to yourself on the Tuesday you're short of cash.

Advance before work, always. 50/25/25 on projects, first month prepaid on retainers, payment due on receipt. Ad spend lives on the client's card, funded 5 days ahead — never yours. Every rule here exists because a solo operator has no accounts department to absorb a bad debt.

Capacity governor. 🟢 ≤4 retainers · 🟡 5–7 → new prices rise 20% and a start-date queue is published · 🔴 8 → the system refuses to assemble another retainer proposal without a written override, and the bench is activated. ninety.

This file is the single honest reason one person can charge what a team charges: the floor of output quality is enforced by a process, not by whether you felt good on Tuesday morning. Draft and judgement are never the same pass.

LAW I

The Floor

₹1L minimum, no trial work, no 15-day tests. The only direction price moves is up.

LAW II

Proof before promise

No number without a screenshot, export or dashboard behind it. No data yet says "no data yet — first read on 14 Sept".

LAW III

Never guess business truths

Budget, access, terms and money come from a signed file in the client's authority folder, or they don't happen.

The human owns the relationship

Discovery calls, proposals, renewals, bad news: the system drafts, a person sends. This row is never automated.

What "free" quietly costs you — know it, then decide.

Free AI tiers may train on what you paste, so client-confidential material gets anonymised or run on a no-training endpoint — and once a client pays, that paid tier becomes a defensible, billable line item. Watermarks and "via gmail.com" notes read as non-premium on the exact surfaces where trust is judged. Free tiers move without notice: in 2026 alone Oracle halved its free ARM allowance, Google set an end date for custom-domain send-as, and Gemini's free Pro access effectively disappeared. All three would have broken a design that copied a 2024 list.

The mitigation is structural, not hopeful: the repo is the source of truth so no vendor holds your company hostage; a weekly AI radar checks limits; nothing critical depends on the free VM; and every daemon's manual twin is already written. Then, when revenue exists, three small purchases retire most of the risk — a paid AI tier, custom-domain sending, Zoho Standard. Free is the starting position, not the religion.

11 · Build order

Ninety days,
one module a week.

Ordered so that money-visible assets exist early — momentum comes from first revenue, not discipline. Constitution before engines, engines before delivery, automation last: you automate a process that already works on paper. Automating a broken process only makes broken faster.

Constitution + agent rules + nine roles + gate. Domain, DNS, custom email. Secrets and quota ledger. Client scaffold script. Proposal, audit, MSA, invoice templates.
