## Demo run · one full cycle, Thursday 18:1x
Purpose: prove the design runs as written before any of it is called a product. Fictional client `ND`,
one intake, one production batch, one report file, one seal. Nothing client-shaped was committed; the demo
vault was deleted afterwards (the public `leads.yml` is `leads: []` again, and the guard confirmed it).

**What the run cost, per ritual** (the real number, not the design's estimate): `intake` 4 min ·
`produce`+gate 24 min · `report` (digest + client file) 19 min · `seal` + `log` 2 min. **≈49 minutes for
one client-week plus one lead.** Six clients a week at that rate is 5 h of system time, which is inside the
7–8 h/week promise — the promise survives contact with a stopwatch.

**Three defects it found, all fixed in this commit:**
1. `public-guard` scanned `.vault-work/` for PII, so every honest session would have gone red for the
   plaintext that is *supposed* to live there. The content scans now skip it; rule 3b still fails the moment
   anything under `.vault-work/` is tracked. Verified both ways.
2. Rule 3c (no client name in the public zone) covered `os/pipeline` and `os/ops` but not `os/memory/` —
   i.e. STATEBOARD and the log, the two files most likely to name a client out of habit. Now covered, and
   the board carries an explicit "code, not name" rule.
3. `os/templates/INDEX.md` told agents to read `clients/<slug>/00-account/MANIFEST.yml`; the record is at
   `clients/<slug>/MANIFEST.yml`. One of those paths would have made every template assembly fail on a
   missing file. Reconciled to the template's actual layout.

**Also verified by doing:** 61 KB client folder → 9,328 bytes sealed; open → 10 files byte-identical
(sha256 of the whole tree matched before and after); wrong passphrase exits 1 rather than printing garbage;
`vault cat` reads a single file without leaving plaintext on disk.
