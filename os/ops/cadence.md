# The Operating Rhythm — 2h 15m/day, then you're done

This file is the answer to your actual request: *"after I got the OS I shouldn't be free and bored, I
should get busy with clients."* Not busier with work — **busier with people.** So the design fixes the
shape of the day before it fixes the tools. A 2-hour operating window is not laziness; it's the forcing
function that makes the system get built properly, because there's nowhere for manual grunt work to hide.

## The day (IST)

| Time | Window | What happens | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| 07:30 | *system already ran* | D01 morning brief lands on your phone | OS |
| 08:00–08:40 | **W1 — Pipeline** | Score new leads, send 5–10 outbound, reply to everything, book calls | you + R7 |
| 08:40–09:00 | triage inbox | approve/decline overnight daemon outputs, no new work | you |
| 11:00–13:00 | **Calls** | discovery/MBR/strategy calls (2 slots, batched) | you |
| 14:00–14:20 | **W2 — Approvals** | gate the day's deliverables, bulk-approve client requests (5–10 min/client) | you + R8 |
| 14:20–14:45 | money | invoices, reminders, proposals sent | R8 drafts, you send |
| 16:00–17:00 | **Client time (real)** | relationship calls, on-screen walks, expansion conversations | you |
| 17:00–17:30 | **W3 — Learn** | D12 items, playbook patch, log line, tomorrow's 1 focus | R9 |
| evening | *protected* | no client comms after 7pm except amber/red incidents | boundary is policy |

Weekly: Mon strategy batch · Tue ads · Wed design · Thu reports · Fri **new business + capacity review**.
Sunday: everything runs automatically; you read one 300-word retro (or don't — that's the point).

## The four numbers you check daily (60 seconds, on your phone)
1. Calls booked this week (target ≥5)
2. Proposals out / awaiting reply (target: nothing older than 12 days)
3. Deliverables awaiting *your* glance (target: zero older than 24h)
4. Cash collected this month vs committed (target: ≥100% of due)

Anything else about the business, ask the OS — it's in the repo.

## Time guards (the difference between "AI agency" and "AI hobby")
- **Never** approve deliverables one at a time. Bulk, once a day. Context switching costs more than the work.
- **Never** do a task the system can do, even "quickly". If you did it 3 times, write the playbook or it goes back into a daemon. The fix is *always* a document.
- **Never** take a call without a prior booking and the prep brief. Unprepared calls are how solo founders end up freelancing again at ₹20k.
- **Always** end a working session by writing the next action into the ticket. You start tomorrow from a file, never from memory.

## The 30-minute weekly "system hour" (Friday, non-negotiable)
One defect fixed in a playbook. One automation extended to one more client. One thing you did manually
this week gets a ticket. 52 weeks × 1 improvement = the compounding asset no competitor can copy,
because they'd need your exact 52 weeks.

## What busy-with-clients should feel like at 6 retainers
~7 client-facing hours/week: two decision-maker calls each, one MBR a month, one renewal conversation a quarter.
Everything else you "should" be doing is either a draft (approve) or a file (already written). If you're
still assembling decks and formatting spreadsheets, that's a daemon not yet built — go build it, then
take the client for coffee.
