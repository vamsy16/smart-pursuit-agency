# Objection Playbook — answer it once, use it for five years

At ₹10L there are only ever 8 objections. Write the *best* answer once; the OS reuses it in proposals,
emails and calls. (Never argue. Agree, reframe, restate the floor, offer the smaller paid thing.)

### 1. "It's too expensive."
> "Probably it is — that's why we're not the first place most teams start. Here's the honest test: what's
> your average deal value, and how many would you need to close to cover ₹1.5L? [Do the maths on the call.]
> If that's 1–2 deals, the question isn't price, it's whether you believe 6 months of this gets you there.
> If it isn't, start with the Sprint at ₹1L and see the system work before you commit a year."
**Never** solve price by lowering price. Solve it by lowering scope (Sprint/Audit) or by naming what
they're already paying for *nothing happening*.

### 2. "We tried an agency before and it didn't work."
Ask three questions, in this order: *What did you pay? What exactly did they deliver? How did you know it
was working in month 1?* Most answers are "retainer, deliverables unclear, we didn't". Then: that's not
an agency problem, it's a measurement problem, and measurement is the first thing we build — before a
single campaign. This is your strongest moment: their bad experience is the spec for your onboarding.

### 3. "Why not just hire in-house?"
> "You should — if one person can do SEO, ads, content, design, reporting and CRO for ₹65L–1.1Cr a year
> and stay 3 years. Hiring is 3 months, ramp is 3 more, and they leave. You get the same output next week
> at a third of the cost, with the whole system documented so it survives me. If in 18 months it's better
> for you to build a team, I'll help you build it — I'd rather be your partner than your employer."
(Saying this out loud is why they trust the number.)

### 4. "Can you guarantee results?"
> "No — and if any agency tells you yes, ask them to put it in writing, then watch them disappear. What I
> can guarantee: volume (47 items/week, itemised), the review cadence, the SLA, and that if we're off
> track you'll hear it from me first. What we can put in writing is a **stop rule**: if by day 90 we've
> missed [2 named leading indicators], we rework the next 90 days at our cost. I'm betting my fee on the
> system, not your hopes."
**Never** promise rankings, ROAS or lead counts. Promise the system, the volume, the SLA, the review.

### 5. "Can we start with just social media at ₹15k?"
> "Not with us — our floor is ₹1L/month, and honestly at that budget you're buying activity, not outcomes.
> Two things that'll help more: our audit at ₹25k, or I'll introduce you to someone on our bench who does
> this well at your level." → Protects your price *and* the relationship. Say the floor twice in any call.

### 6. "My market is different / our industry can't be marketed like that."
> "Usually 'different' means 'no one's explained it to me in a way that fit'. Tell me the specific
> constraint — the buying cycle, the regulation, the decision committee — and if I can't design around it,
> I'll say so on this call." 9/10 times the constraint is real and becomes a line in the plan. 1/10 it's a
> polite no, and you saved both sides 6 months.

### 7. "I need my partner's / CFO's approval."
> "Of course. Let's make that easy: here's the 2-page version built for them — cost of inaction, what's in
> scope, what's out, the stop rule, and the payment terms. Want me to walk them through it for 20 minutes?"
Then **ask for the meeting**. A "no" you can answer; a silence you can't. And if you can't reach them,
you have an internal champion with no authority — that's a Tier-B deal, treat it like one.

### 8. "We'll do it ourselves with AI."
> "Yes — you should. Half of what you'd be paying agencies for, AI now does free. What it won't do: know
> your margin, own the outcome, notice the leak you don't know about, or be accountable on a call when a
> number is flat. We are not selling you AI output. We're selling you judgement, a delivery line that runs
> every week whether anyone remembers, and one throat to choke. If you'd rather run it yourself with our
> playbook, the Audit + a 2-hour handover is ₹25k and you keep everything."
This answer is also why the OS is a *selling point* and not a secret: "we built an AI operating system
so one senior person can out-produce a department" is a reason to pay more, not less.

## Universal close
Every objection ends the same way: **a smaller paid next step, with a date.** Never "let me know".
