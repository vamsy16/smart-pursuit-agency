# Price Card — Smart Pursuit Agency (v1, 2026)

> **Publish this.** A public price card is the cheapest premium-positioning lever that exists: it
> deletes the wrong 80% of enquiries in four seconds and makes the right 20% assume the floor is real.
> An agency that hides prices gets price-shopped. An agency that states a floor gets treated as a vendor
> with standards.

## Floor (absolute, no exceptions)
`min project ₹1,00,000 · min retainer ₹1,00,000/month · min term 6 months · media budget on client, ≥₹1L/mo recommended`

The number that closes is not "marketing is expensive". It's *"you're comparing us to a department, not to
a freelancer."* Never quote competitor prices; quote the **alternative the client is already paying for** —

## 5. Rules
1. **Never discount. Reduce scope.** "At ₹2.5L we can't include the 12 social posts" — not "okay, ₹2L".
2. Price on **value + capacity**, never on client's revenue. Asking "what's your turnover?" invites
   lowballing; asking "what's this worth if it works?" invites it not to.
3. Annual prepay = 2 months free *only* if it converts a 6-month to a 12-month. Never for a shorter term.
4. Fee rises 10–15%/yr at renewal for existing clients (they're used to us; the alternative is painful).
   New clients see today's rates from the published card — one price, always. Public pricing removes
   the negotiation and the resentment.
5. Ad spend, stock, licences, printing, influencer fees = client's account, always. We never front money.
6. Any offer below the floor goes to a **partner on the Bench** (referral, no commission taken) — polite,
   helpful, and it protects the price.

## 6. Numbers that keep you honest (D10 tracks these)
Target blended **₹1.6L+/client/month** · average project ≥₹9L · gross margin ≥**85%** (bench cost is the
only variable) · **collection cycle ≤5 days** · ≥70% of revenue on retainers (project revenue is spiky;
retainers are what lets you sleep, and what you actually sell the client) · pipeline coverage ≥6 months of outflow.
