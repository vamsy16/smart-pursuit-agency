## 5. The Lead Magnet — "DBA Fit Report" (fixes the giveaway problem)

The old offer was a checklist. **The video already gave away the checklist.** This replaces it with something 53 seconds cannot deliver:

**One page, personalised, delivered within 24 hours:**
1. Which **one** of the four programs matches your degree, experience and current status
2. **Your** intake window and application deadline
3. Tuition for that program + funding routes (scholarships, payment plans, sponsor documents)
4. That school's **exact practicum/CPT policy language, in writing**
5. **Your employer's paperwork checklist** — what HR and the DSO have to sign, and when

> **The rule:** if a prospect could Google it, it belongs in the video. **If it requires their degree dates, their status and their employer's name, it belongs in the Fit Report.** That is the only thing worth a phone number.

**Then the disclosure, in the same thread:** *"One thing up front: I'm an education advisor, not an immigration attorney. CPT is authorized by your school's DSO. For advice on your status, speak to a licensed attorney."* Sending this **first** builds more trust than sending it late.

## 7. Proof Rules (fix the "no social proof" flaw without inventing any)

The names and numbers in Script A are placeholders. **Handle them in this order:**

1. **Real number of students placed** → use it. A specific number ("[X] students") beats any adjective.
2. **No track record yet?** → delete the number and use verifiable third-party proof instead: the SEVP memo date, the universities' own published outcomes, your own name and credentials, years of experience.
3. **Real student case** → get written consent, then use: situation → what was verified → outcome. **One real case outperforms ten adjectives.**
4. **Never** write a testimonial you can't produce a name for. One fabricated case is an unrecoverable brand risk, and this audience cross-checks everything.

## 9. Writer's Notes — Voice Discipline

- **Rahul (A):** senior advisor, slightly annoyed on his behalf. Urgent but never frantic.
- **Sneha (B):** calm, foresighted. She is being *rescued from a future problem*, not sold to.
- **Arjun (C):** peer-to-peer, career framing. Never mention fear of status loss to this viewer.
- **Vikram (D):** flat, plain, no adjectives. Every soft word costs credibility.
- **Every script:** the disclaimer line is the *quietest* line, never the loudest. Its power comes from being said calmly while everyone else is shouting.
