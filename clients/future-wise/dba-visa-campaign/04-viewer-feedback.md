## 2. The Five Moments That Nearly Lost Me (ranked by damage)

### 🥇 #1 — Second 38: "Wait… what is Future Wise?"
I've heard four university names and three compliance questions, and I still don't know **who is talking to me or what they sell.** Agent? Consultancy? University itself? Lead-gen site? Ambiguity at the exact moment I'm asked to act is the single biggest leak. Every other ad in this niche at least tells me what it is.

### 🥇 #2 — Second 38–47: "But I already *have* the checklist."
You gave me all three questions **in the video**. Then you asked me to hand over my phone number for "the checklist." I just heard the checklist. You've given the value away and then asked me to pay for it again in the only currency you wanted — my contact details. This is the deepest structural flaw in the script. The lead magnet must contain something the 52 seconds *cannot* deliver: **my** program match, **my** intake date, **my** employer's paperwork.

### 🥈 #3 — Second 13: the vague audit line
"Programs are being audited now — some students found out after they'd paid." Which programs? Audited by whom? No specifics = reads as manufactured urgency. But the *next* line (the dated SEVP memo) is specific and verifiable and I believe it instantly. **You don't need the vague line at all.**

### 🥈 #4 — No social proof, no credentials
An anonymous voice giving immigration-adjacent advice on a $50k decision. There is not one concrete proof point: no name, no student count, no case, no face I can attribute. In a high-anxiety, high-cost purchase, absence of proof is read as absence of substance.

### 🥉 #5 — "Comment 'DBA'"
See Rahul. You're asking for a **public** act of vulnerability from people whose entire problem is that they need to appear stable and employable. The comment CTA is great for cold reach and terrible for your most qualified viewers.

**Honourable mentions:** no tuition ballpark (I assume I can't afford it and leave); no "here's what happens after you DM me" (uncertainty = friction); "it's in your DMs in 30 seconds" is a promise nobody measures — if you reply in two hours, you've lost me twice over.

## 3. What's Genuinely Working (don't touch these)

- **"Don't pay a university a dollar yet."** Best line in the script. It inverts the category — every competitor is selling, you're telling me to stop. Instant credibility spike.
- **The three questions.** Specific, repeatable, screenshot-able, low-effort. This is the reason I'd trust you at all.
- **The dated SEVP reference.** Verifiable, one month old, makes you the best-informed person in my feed.
- **"Your school authorizes CPT, not us."** The most credible sentence in the script. Keep it verbatim.
- **The 52-second runtime.** Correct. I don't feel trapped.

**One expiry warning:** the August 12 news hook has a shelf life of roughly 6–10 weeks before it reads as recycled. Script C is your evergreen — rotate into it before fatigue, don't after.

## 4. The V2 Script — with fixes applied

Changes: identity by second 12 · audit line cut · lead magnet made non-Googleable · tuition transparency · **private CTA offered alongside the public one** · proof point added.

**On-screen CTA bar must change to:** `Comment "DBA" · or DM (private) → free program match`

## 5. What To Do Instead of Trusting Me

My persona verdicts are reasoning, not evidence. Do this instead:

1. **Run the 8 hooks with equal spend.** If hook #1 ("H-1B not picked?") beats hook #5, the anxiety angle is validated — my read of Vikram was wrong.
2. **Run V1 vs V2 as a split.** Same creative, two CTAs: comment-only vs comment+DM. If DM volume spikes while comment volume holds, FIX #1 was real.
3. **Track qualified rate by hook, not lead volume.** The persona that converts most may not be the persona that enrolls.
4. **Read the DMs literally.** The most valuable output of this campaign is the exact sentence prospects use when they open the conversation. That sentence should become your next hook — and it will be better than anything in my hook bank.

**If one thing in this document is right, it's this:** your best viewer is *afraid to raise their hand in public*. Give them a quiet door.
