## 2. Three Full Content Briefs

### BRIEF #1 — "The DBA Documentation File" *(priority)*

- **Target reader:** Rahul — OPT running, comparing programs, already slightly suspicious of the whole category. He has read enough Reddit threads to be scared and has no idea what to do with that fear.
- **The ONE belief this piece must change:**
  > *"The documents I collect **before** I enrol are what protect me **later** — and I can ask for every one of them in writing."*
- **Why this belief:** it converts a passive researcher into an active collector, and the collection process is exactly what generates a qualified lead. It is also the single most defensible position in the market — every competitor promises an outcome; this piece is the only one that describes evidence.
- **Structure (H2s):**
  1. What "an integral part of an established curriculum" actually means — with the regulation cited
  2. The five documents to collect *before* you pay a deposit
  3. The email to send your DSO — exact wording, and why you want it in writing
  4. What your employer has to sign, and when
  5. What gets asked for later: the pattern in document requests
  6. What this file does not do — it isn't legal advice and it isn't a guarantee
  7. Your next step
- **Sources to cite (real, verifiable):** 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(i); the SEVP broadcast of 12 Aug 2026; each university's own published program page for practicum and residency requirements.
- **Distribution plan:** homepage link from every Reel's link-in-bio · PDF version becomes the Fit Report's first attachment · LinkedIn long post · WhatsApp broadcast in 5 parts · required reading before any advisor call.

---

### BRIEF #2 — "Four DBAs, One Question" *(comparison page)*

- **Target reader:** Vikram — has heard the pitch, has a lawyer, trusts nobody, compares everything.
- **The ONE belief this piece must change:**
  > *"The right program is decided by its practical-training structure — not by its tuition, its ranking, or what a counsellor tells me on a call."*
- **Structure (H2s):**
  1. The only question that decides this comparison
  2. The four programs at a glance — format, residency frequency, length, credits
  3. How each handles practical training — **and what you must verify with each school directly**
  4. Tuition and the total cost shape
  5. Where the risk sits in each model
  6. How to verify all of this yourself in 20 minutes
  7. What we won't claim
- **Critical rule:** state facts about each university with the date verified and a link to the university's own page. Where a detail is unconfirmed, **say so on the page.** A comparison that admits its own gaps is more persuasive to a sceptic than one that appears complete.
- **Distribution plan:** targets program-comparison search intent · linked from the Fit Report · repurposed into the carousel and the 30-day calendar.

### BRIEF #3 — "What the August 2026 SEVP Memo Does and Doesn't Change" *(awareness)*

- **Target reader:** Sneha — graduating in ten months, planning ahead, has seen alarming headlines in group chats and wants the straight version.
- **The ONE belief this piece must change:**
  > *"This is a documentation standard, not a ban — so the correct response is to collect evidence, not to panic or to trust whoever promises the most."*
- **Structure (H2s):**
  1. What SEVP actually sent on 12 August 2026
  2. What it restates — the existing regulation, unchanged
  3. What it does **not** do — it is not a rule and not a ban on Day-1 CPT
  4. Why the distinction matters to you specifically
  5. The documentation test that follows from it
  6. What to do next
- **This piece is the writer's tightrope.** Overstate the memo and you're fear-mongering; understate it and you're misleading. Both destroy credibility with the only audience worth having. The headline must be accurate, not exciting.
- **Distribution plan:** the reference piece every other asset links to · pinned social post · the "read this first" in the DM auto-reply · refreshed each intake cycle.

## 3. Sample Opening — Brief #1, first 150 words

> **If you enrol in a doctoral program and it goes wrong later, the problem will not be the school. It will be the folder you didn't keep.**
>
> That's the uncomfortable lesson of the last six months. On 12 August 2026, SEVP told every designated school official in the country that it is seeing a rise in practical-training authorisations that don't meet the regulatory standard — training that isn't "an integral part of an established curriculum" — and that it is increasing scrutiny of those approvals. It reminded DSOs that when they sign your I-20, they certify that information under penalty of perjury.
>
> Read that again, because most people miss the consequence: the burden of proof doesn't land on the school. It lands on you, years later, in an H-1B or green-card file, when an officer asks what your practical training actually was.
>
> This guide is the folder you should have kept. Five documents and one email, before you pay anything.

*(160 words. Two-sentence hook, no throat-clearing, states the stakes before the solution — and every claim traces to §2's cited sources.)*
