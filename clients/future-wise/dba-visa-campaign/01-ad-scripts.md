# Future Wise — DBA Campaign: Ad Script Pack
**Offer:** DBA (Doctor of Business Administration) — IT track & Management track
**Audience:** F-1 / OPT / STEM-OPT / H-1B-not-selected professionals in the US (Indian & South-Asian diaspora first)
**Platform:** Meta Reels/Feed (primary), YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn, TikTok
**Goal:** 200 highly-qualified leads per month
**Built with:** `04-service-delivery/video-production.md` → Hook Bank → 3 Full Scripts → Editing Brief → Repurposing Plan

---

## 0. The One Belief Every Script Must Change

> **"Any DBA will solve my status problem."** → **"Only a DBA whose practical training is *built into the curriculum* will hold up — and I must verify that before I pay."**

That belief shift is the whole campaign. It converts, because it flips the viewer from *shopping for a degree* to *checking their own risk*. A viewer checking their own risk fills a form — that is your lead.

**Hard constraint:** the master script reads in **52 seconds** at a standard 175 wpm short-form pace — every word earning its place, with a documented cut-line (see §2) if your presenter reads slower.

## 1. Hook Bank (8 angles — test all 8 in week 1)

| # | Angle | Hook (first 1.5 seconds) | Why it stops the scroll |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Direct identification** | "H-1B not picked? On OPT with the clock running? Don't pay any university a dollar yet." | Names the exact moment of pain, in their words |
| 2 | **News / authority** | "August 12, 2026. SEVP sent a memo to every school in America. If you're planning a CPT program, read it first." | Real, dated, verifiable event — feels like insider news |
| 3 | **Contrarian** | "A DBA won't fix your visa. A badly chosen DBA can end it. Here's the difference." | Breaks the category's promise |
| 4 | **Curiosity gap** | "I've reviewed hundreds of DBA applications. The students who get burned never ask question two." | Open loop, promise of a specific question |
| 5 | **Loss framing** | "The students hit hardest in 2026 were the ones who checked nothing before they enrolled." | Loss aversion beats gain framing for risk decisions |
| 6 | **Numbers / specificity** | "Three questions. Ask them before you pay. Question two is the one that gets people denied later." | Specific, low-effort, high-stakes |
| 7 | **Relatable scenario** | "OPT ends in 90 days. Your manager wants to keep you. Here's the decision tree." | Puts them inside a real deadline |
| 8 | **Comparison** | "Four universities. Four CPT policies. Only one of them matters for your H-1B file." | Comparison content is the highest-intent format in this niche |

**Kill rule:** any hook under **20% 3-second hook rate** after 1,500 impressions gets cut. No sentiment, no exceptions.

## 2. MASTER SCRIPT — "The August Memo" (52 seconds)

**Format:** 9:16 vertical, 1080×1920. Talking head + screen capture + text cards.
**Word count:** **153 words.** At a standard short-form pace of 175 wpm that reads in **52 seconds** — a comfortable margin under 60s. If your presenter delivers slower, see the cut-line note below the table.
**Delivery note:** do NOT shout. This audience is anxious and financially exposed. The tone that converts here is a **senior advisor who is slightly annoyed on their behalf** — "why is nobody telling you this?"

| Time | Spoken (VO) | On-screen text | Shot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:04** *(13 words)* | "H-1B not picked? OPT clock running? Don't pay a university a dollar yet." | **H-1B not picked? ⏳** / "Watch this before you enroll" | Tight talking head, direct eyeline. First frame is the face, not a logo. |
| **0:04–0:13** *(26 words)* | "August 12, 2026 — SEVP tightened CPT scrutiny. Practical training has to be an integral part of your curriculum. Not a job permit with a tuition bill." | "Aug 12, 2026 — SEVP tightens CPT scrutiny" + highlighted quote: *"integral part of an established curriculum"* | Screen capture of the memo headline. Hand pointing at the line. |
| **0:13–0:18** *(13 words)* | "Programs are being audited now — some students found out after they'd paid." | "Audits are live." / "Some found out after paying." | Cut to b-roll: student at laptop, tuition invoice, empty classroom. |
| **0:18–0:28** *(29 words)* | "A DBA can be that bridge, if it's built right. Online coursework, one campus visit a term. IT track or management track. Indiana Wesleyan, Westcliff, Sofia, National Louis." | Four name cards (no logos): **Indiana Wesleyan · Westcliff · Sofia · National Louis** then **IT Track / Management Track** | Name cards animate in on beat. Split screen IT vs Management. |
| **0:28–0:38** *(29 words)* | "Three questions before you enroll. One — is the practicum required for every student? Two — is it tied to your major? Three — is the CPT policy in writing?" | **1. Required for all? 2. Tied to your major? 3. In writing?** | Big numeral cards, 3s each. Hard cuts on each number. |
| **0:38–0:47** *(27 words)* | "Future Wise runs all three checks free: comparison, compliance checklist, intake dates. And to be clear — your school authorizes CPT, not us. That's why you verify first." | "Free: Program + Compliance Review" / "CPT is authorized by your school's DSO — not by us." | Advisor-to-camera. Calm, trustworthy delivery on the disclaimer line. |
| **0:47–0:52** *(16 words)* | "Comment 'DBA' — it's in your DMs in 30 seconds. Or tap the link." | **COMMENT "DBA"** (persistent bottom bar from 0:00) / "DM in 30 seconds" | End card holds 3s. Keyword stays visible. |

> **If your presenter reads slower than 170 wpm:** cut the 0:13–0:18 line *("Programs are being audited now…")* and go straight from the SEVP line into the DBA bridge. That removes 13 words and about 5 seconds of runtime. **Never cut the three questions, the university names, or the disclaimer line** — those are the conversion engine and the compliance floor.

**Persistent elements (whole video):**
- Bottom safe-area bar: `Comment "DBA" → free compliance checklist`
- Burned-in captions, 2–3 words per card, never covering the bottom 420px
- Low music bed, ducks under VO; one sound-design hit on the "three questions" reveal

**Why the disclaimer line *increases* conversions:** saying "your school authorizes CPT, not us" is the single most credible sentence in the script. Every competitor promises; you're the one telling the truth. That is what makes a nervous, high-value prospect trust you with their phone number.

## 7. Editing Brief (hand this to the editor as-is)

- **Specs:** 1080×1920, 9:16, H.264, ≤30 MB. Safe zones: top 250px, bottom 420px, right 120px untouched.
- **Pacing:** cut every **1.5–2.5 seconds**. No shot longer than 3s except the final end card (3s hold).
- **Captions:** burned in, 2–3 words per card, heavy sans-serif, white with black stroke, keyword CTA highlighted in one accent colour used nowhere else in the video.
- **Audio:** VO at −3dB peak; music bed at −22dB, ducks 4dB under VO. One riser + impact on the "three questions" reveal. No trending audio with lyrics on the compliance line — it must be heard clearly.
- **Text discipline:** on-screen text must be *readable at a glance* — no paragraphs, max 7 words per card.
- **End card:** 3s, keyword + "Free" + logo (small, bottom third). Never end on a black frame.
- **Deliverables per script:** 1:1, 4:5, 9:16 masters + SRT file.
